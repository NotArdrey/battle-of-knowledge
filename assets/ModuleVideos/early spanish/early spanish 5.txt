Although the victory at Mactan showed that Filipinos could resist foreign powers, it did not stop Spanish colonization. After the battle, Magellan’s remaining crew left the Philippines, but Spain continued to pursue control of the islands. In 1543, the archipelago was named Las Islas Filipinas in honor of King Philip II. The turning point came in 1565, when Miguel López de Legazpi established permanent Spanish settlements, and by 1571, Manila became the colonial capital, beginning over three centuries of Spanish rule that reshaped Philippine society and culture.
